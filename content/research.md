<div class="page-heading"><h1>Research.</h1><p class="lead">Speech recognition, programming languages and the systems that turn computation into useful software.</p><div class="square" aria-hidden="true"></div></div><section class="research-speech" aria-labelledby="speech-h"><div class="section-title"><h2 id="speech-h">Speech &amp;<br>machine learning</h2><p class="body">The research behind avette starts with a practical problem: making speech recognition work well with the way people actually talk, in more languages, on their own devices.</p></div><div class="speech-notes"><div><h3>Data that sounds like people</h3><p>We are developing a speech corpus with consent, with collection in Europe. The goal is training data that reflects real speech, rather than relying only on audiobooks or parliamentary recordings.</p></div><div><h3>From training to the device</h3><p>Our work includes fine-tuning open speech models, GPU training with CUDA, and running recognition locally. Current applications are dictation on the Mac; further work includes iPhone input, transcription and live translation.</p></div></div><p class="research-note">Research in progress. A paper on the speech recognition work is in draft.</p></section><section class="computation" aria-labelledby="computation-h"><div class="section-title"><h2 id="computation-h">Languages &amp;<br>computation</h2><p class="body">vix explores how to describe computation so that dependencies, reuse and execution are part of the system, rather than conventions every project has to reinvent.</p></div><div class="computation-content"><div class="dependency-diagram" role="img" aria-label="Conceptual dependency graph: a changed input affects one computation and its result, while an independent branch can be reused."><svg viewBox="0 0 560 280" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" aria-hidden="true"><g fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2"><path d="M90 70H240L410 140M90 210H240L410 140"/></g><g fill="var(--egg)" stroke="var(--blue)" stroke-width="3"><circle cx="80" cy="210" r="18"/><rect x="190" y="188" width="90" height="44"/></g><g fill="var(--red)"><circle cx="80" cy="70" r="18"/><rect x="190" y="48" width="90" height="44"/><circle cx="430" cy="140" r="24"/></g><g fill="currentColor" font-size="16" text-anchor="middle"><text x="80" y="30">Changed input</text><text x="235" y="30">Recompute</text><text x="80" y="263">Unchanged input</text><text x="235" y="263">Reuse</text><text x="443" y="198">Result</text></g></svg><p>One changed input, only the dependent work repeated. A conceptual illustration, not a benchmark.</p></div><div class="body"><h3>Beyond a build system</h3><p>A language and platform for general incremental recomputation. Build systems and faster CI are applications of that idea, not its boundary.</p><h3>Correctness across machines</h3><p>The work spans dependency tracking, caching, sandboxed execution and distributed builds. Rust and memory safety are a foundation; we also want correctness in the way work is described and reused.</p><a class="text-link" href="https://vix.works">More about vix</a></div></div></section>
